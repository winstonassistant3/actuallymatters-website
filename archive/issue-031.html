TOP SIGNALS THAT MATTER

THE CONTROL PLANE WAR — AI'S NEXT BATTLEGROUND ISN'T MODELS, IT'S INFRASTRUCTURE

WHAT IS HAPPENING: Microsoft and OpenAI currently lead enterprise agent orchestration deployments, but new VB Pulse data shows Anthropic establishing its first measurable foothold in the infrastructure layer where AI agents actually run. Separately, Intercom launched Fin Operator — an AI agent whose sole function is managing another AI agent — marking the first time a major customer service platform has attempted this at scale.

WHY NOW: The model performance race has plateaued enough that enterprise buyers are asking different questions. They don't want better chatbots — they want systems that can coordinate multiple agents, manage handoffs, and maintain context across workflows.

WHY IT MATTERS: For two years, enterprise AI has been framed as a model quality contest. That was the wrong frame. The real value — and the real lock-in — sits one layer up: in the orchestration infrastructure that routes tasks, manages state, and coordinates between agents. Whoever controls that layer controls enterprise AI deployment, regardless of which foundation model runs underneath.

WHAT THIS MEANS: The companies building agent control planes today are building the operating systems of enterprise AI. This is the AWS moment of the agent era — infrastructure that becomes invisible but indispensable.

WHAT MOST PEOPLE MISS: Agent orchestration infrastructure creates switching costs that foundation models never could. You can swap out Claude for GPT-4 in an afternoon. Ripping out the orchestration layer that routes your customer service tickets, manages escalations, and maintains conversation state across six different agents? That's a nine-month migration project. Microsoft and OpenAI understand this. Anthropic just figured it out.

WHAT YOU SHOULD DO:

  • If you are a professional → Learn orchestration frameworks (LangGraph, AutoGen, CrewAI) — not just prompt engineering. The valuable skill is system design, not model wrangling.
  • If you are a founder → Build on orchestration platforms with the weakest lock-in. Avoid proprietary agent frameworks from model providers — you're one acquisition away from being trapped.
  • If you are an investor → Map the orchestration layer. The companies building horizontal agent infrastructure (not vertical AI apps) are building the next Twilio or Stripe.

THE CALL: Everyone's still arguing about which model is 2% better at reasoning. Meanwhile, Microsoft is quietly building the plumbing that every enterprise AI deployment will run through. By the time people realize orchestration matters more than models, the game will already be over.

CHINA'S CLEAN ENERGY SPENDING GAP JUST BECAME STRUCTURAL

WHAT IS HAPPENING: China outspent the rest of the world combined on clean energy in 2025, according to Atlas Public Policy. Separately, Canada and Alberta reached a carbon pricing deal that could finally move a long-discussed West Coast oil pipeline into construction by September 2027. Goldman Sachs added small modular reactors (SMRs) to its nuclear model and now projects 17% upside in uranium demand.

WHY NOW: China's clean energy investment isn't slowing — it's accelerating. While Western governments debate subsidies and permitting reform, Beijing is building at scale. The Canada pipeline deal matters because it shows fossil fuel infrastructure is still getting financed and built, just with longer timelines and higher political friction.

WHY IT MATTERS: The energy transition isn't happening uniformly. China is building the manufacturing base and supply chains for solar, batteries, and EVs faster than the West can design permitting processes. This isn't a temporary spending surge — it's industrial policy creating durable competitive advantage.

WHAT THIS MEANS: By the time Western economies finish debating how to build clean energy infrastructure, China will control the upstream supply chains and have a decade of operational learning. The gap isn't closing — it's widening.

WHAT MOST PEOPLE MISS: China's clean energy dominance isn't just about solar panels and batteries. It's about locking in the next 30 years of energy infrastructure dependencies. Every country that buys Chinese EVs, batteries, or grid equipment is buying into a Chinese-designed energy system. That's geopolitical leverage that compounds annually. Meanwhile, the West is still building oil pipelines with seven-year lead times.

WHAT YOU SHOULD DO:

  • If you are a professional → Develop expertise in Chinese energy supply chains — knowing where components actually come from matters more than policy white papers.
  • If you are a founder → Build for a world where Chinese clean energy tech is the default, not the exception. Integration and service layers around Chinese hardware are underpriced opportunities.
  • If you are an investor → Overweight uranium and nuclear infrastructure. It's the only clean energy category where the West still has structural advantages and China hasn't built insurmountable scale.

THE CALL: The West is treating the energy transition like a policy debate. China is treating it like an industrial mobilization. One of these approaches builds power plants. The other builds Substack essays.

BERKSHIRE'S AIRLINE RETURN SIGNALS THE ROTATION INTO OPERATIONAL LEVERAGE

WHAT IS HAPPENING: Berkshire Hathaway built a $2.6B position in Delta Air Lines during Q1 2026, making it the conglomerate's 14th-largest holding. This marks Berkshire's return to airlines after Warren Buffett famously exited the sector during COVID, calling it a mistake to ever invest in airlines. Separately, Bill Ackman's Pershing Square disclosed a new Microsoft position, built during the Q1 sell-off.

WHY NOW: Berkshire doesn't chase momentum — it buys operational leverage at reasonable prices. Airlines are now profitable, consolidated, and trading below historical multiples despite strong demand. Ackman's Microsoft bet during a sell-off follows the same logic: buying durable cash flow machines when sentiment turns negative.

WHY IT MATTERS: The smartest capital allocators are rotating out of pure growth stories and into businesses with pricing power, operational leverage, and the ability to return cash. This isn't a defensive move — it's a bet that the next phase of the cycle rewards execution over narrative.

WHAT THIS MEANS: The market is repricing operational quality. Companies that can grow earnings without raising capital, that have pricing power in inflationary environments, and that generate cash they can return to shareholders are getting revalued upward. High-growth, capital-intensive stories are getting revalued downward.

WHAT MOST PEOPLE MISS: Berkshire's airline return isn't about airlines — it's about industry structure. The sector consolidated, capacity discipline improved, and pricing power returned. Buffett didn't change his mind about airlines. The industry changed its behavior. The same pattern is playing out across industrials, energy, and infrastructure. Investors still anchored to 2019 valuations are missing that these businesses fundamentally restructured during COVID and now operate with better economics.

WHAT YOU SHOULD DO:

  • If you are a professional → Study industry consolidation patterns — the next wave of value creation is happening in "boring" sectors that restructured and now have pricing power.
  • If you are a founder → Build businesses that can raise prices without losing customers. Pricing power is the only moat that matters in an inflationary environment.
  • If you are an investor → Rotate into operational leverage. The companies that can grow earnings 15% while growing revenue 8% will outperform the companies growing revenue 40% while burning cash.

THE CALL: Buffett buying airlines again is the market equivalent of your parents getting back together. You thought it would never happen, but the underlying dynamics changed enough that it actually makes sense. Pay attention to what changed, not just that it happened.

MARKET MOVES THAT MATTER

The Roundhill Memory ETF (DRAM) hit $10B in assets at the fastest pace ever recorded for an ETF, driven by what analysts are calling "the biggest bottleneck in the AI buildout." High-bandwidth memory is now the constraint on AI infrastructure deployment, not compute chips. This matters because it shifts the value capture point in the AI stack — away from Nvidia's GPUs and toward memory manufacturers like SK Hynix and Micron.

Stock markets posted their longest weekly winning streak in over a year despite accelerating inflation and rising interest rate expectations. The S&P 500 is up on strong corporate earnings, but the rally is narrow — driven by companies with pricing power and operational leverage, not broad-based growth. This is a quality rotation disguised as a bull market.

Trump disclosed major tech stock purchases in Q1 ethics filings: Amazon, Meta, Oracle, Broadcom, Motorola, and Dell. The pattern is clear — he's buying cloud infrastructure, AI compute, and enterprise software. Whether this is personal conviction or political signaling, it's directionally correct. The administration is betting on the same sectors driving corporate capex.

OPPORTUNITIES

  • Agent orchestration infrastructure — the companies building horizontal platforms for multi-agent coordination are building the next Twilio. Look for startups focused on state management, handoff protocols, and cross-agent context preservation.
  • Nuclear supply chain plays — Goldman's 17% uranium demand upside is conservative. SMRs are moving from PowerPoint to procurement. Uranium miners, enrichment facilities, and nuclear engineering services are all underpriced relative to the buildout timeline.
  • Memory and high-bandwidth interconnects — DRAM's record inflows are early. The AI infrastructure bottleneck has shifted from compute to memory bandwidth. Companies solving data movement problems will capture more value than companies adding more GPUs.
  • Operational leverage in consolidated industries — airlines, industrials, energy services. Look for sectors that restructured during COVID, now have pricing power, and are trading below historical multiples despite improved economics.
  • Chinese clean energy integration and service layers — the hardware is already Chinese. The opportunity is building the software, integration, and service businesses on top of that infrastructure.

MARKET POSITIONING

OVERWEIGHT:

  • High-bandwidth memory manufacturers (SK Hynix, Micron)
  • Uranium miners and nuclear infrastructure
  • Agent orchestration platforms (private markets)
  • Airlines and consolidated industrials with pricing power
  • Microsoft and cloud infrastructure providers

NEUTRAL:

  • Foundation model providers (competitive moat unclear; orchestration layer matters more)
  • Western clean energy hardware manufacturers (structurally disadvantaged vs. China)
  • Traditional SaaS without AI integration (being repriced downward)
  • Oil and gas majors (still profitable but facing longer-term structural headwinds)

AVOID:

  • High-growth, capital-intensive AI startups without clear path to profitability
  • Vertical AI applications without defensible data moats
  • Western solar and battery manufacturers competing directly with Chinese scale
  • Consumer discretionary without pricing power in inflationary environment
  • Anything dependent on permitting timelines improving in the West

BOTTOM LINE

The AI infrastructure war just shifted from models to orchestration, China's clean energy lead is now structural, and the smartest capital allocators are rotating into operational leverage. The next phase of this cycle rewards execution over narrative — and the companies building invisible infrastructure over the ones building viral demos.
