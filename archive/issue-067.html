SECTION 2 — SIGNALS

The zoning board is now the binding constraint on compute

WHAT HAPPENED: The NYT/Siena poll found 61% of 1,503 likely voters oppose data center construction for AI (The Verge). In the same 48 hours: Philadelphia officials floated a site in a neighbourhood already scarred by a defunct oil refinery (TechCrunch); a Secaucus, NJ data center spilled 5,000 gallons of diesel into a Hackensack River tributary (NYT); and Steve Bannon shared a Washington stage with Bernie Sanders to demand AI reforms (NYT). Jensen Huang chose this week to argue AI needs no regulation because safety can be engineered product by product (TechCrunch).

WHY THIS WEEK: A fresh national poll number landed with a midterm cycle underway, and a physical spill gave local opponents a photograph instead of a projection.

THE SECOND-ORDER READ: The industry is arguing about model safety while the losses are accruing in siting, water, diesel tanks, and electricity bills. Huang's "leave it to us" frame is designed for a federal debate about existential risk; it has no answer for a Philadelphia councilmember whose constituents already hosted a refinery. That mismatch matters because the marginal gigawatt of AI capacity is approved locally, and one projection has US data centers consuming more natural gas than Germany and Japan combined by 2035 (TechCrunch). When the Bannon and Sanders coalitions align on a target, the eventual policy is usually a cost — impact fees, ratepayer ring-fencing, interconnection queues — not a ban. Price compute accordingly.

WHAT I'M NOT CERTAIN OF: Poll opposition to an abstraction is cheap, and I can't see from the summary how the question was worded or whether opposition holds when jobs and tax revenue are named. Nor can I verify the gas-consumption projection's assumptions; it is a forecast, not a meter reading.

WHAT TO DO:

  • Operator → If your roadmap assumes a specific region's capacity delivering in 2027, get the interconnection and permit status in writing from your provider this month.
  • Founder → Model a compute cost curve that rises, not falls, through 2027; if your unit economics only work on cheap inference, say so to your board now.
  • Investor → Underwrite power and land entitlement risk as a political variable in data center and adjacent infrastructure exposure, not an engineering one.

THE CALL: Local consent is the scarce input in AI now, and the industry's chosen spokesmen are arguing the wrong case.

SECTION 3 — MARKET MOVES

Oil fell Wednesday despite a pipeline attack, because an unexpected US crude inventory build outweighed the supply scare (CNBC). The mechanism worth holding onto: at these levels price is mostly insurance against a Hormuz closure, not a barrel shortage. Insurance premiums can vanish in a session. Anyone hedging physical exposure should be buying optionality, not chasing spot.

SECTION 5 — BOTTOM LINE

AI's constraints have moved from technical to physical, political, and financial: power and permits, 61% voter opposition, and a cost of capital rising on oil and war spending. The models keep improving; the inputs keep getting more expensive and more contested. Anyone still underwriting compute as a deflationary input is using a 2024 spreadsheet.
