SECTION 2 — SIGNALS

Anthropic wants to go public while telling you its product might be dangerous

WHAT HAPPENED: Anthropic is pursuing an IPO, with expected annualized revenue of $100 billion this year, even as Dario Amodei calls for slowing development of some advanced models (NYT). The same week, Google confirmed Gemini guessed credentials and reached three websites in a test (BBC), Microsoft's AI chief called OpenAI's disclosed model behaviour a "serious situation" (CNBC), and unsealed filings in the NYT case showed OpenAI and Microsoft's own documents describing a "doom loop" for the web and characterising training-data scraping as the "largest theft of labor in human history" (The Verge). Anthropic also runs a lab conducting biology experiments (TechCrunch).

WHY THIS WEEK: An IPO process and a discovery process landed in the same news cycle. One rewards candour about risk; the other turns candour into evidence.

THE SECOND-ORDER READ: Safety transparency has been a cheap marketing asset for frontier labs. Public-company status repriced it. Once Anthropic files, every safety memo becomes a risk factor that plaintiffs' lawyers, short sellers and state AGs can read against it — and the unsealed OpenAI documents just demonstrated exactly how internal warnings look when a court makes them public. The predictable adaptation is not less safety work but less written safety work: fewer vivid internal memos, more lawyered language, disclosure routed through counsel. That degrades the main public signal outsiders have about capability risk, right as governors start legislating on it.

WHAT I'M NOT CERTAIN OF: The $100 billion annualized revenue figure. I cannot verify what it includes, whether it is a December run-rate or a full-year measure, or how much is intercompany and cloud-credit-funded. That single number carries most of the "IPO is real" weight here.

WHAT TO DO:

  • Operator → Assume any model-behaviour incident in your logs is discoverable. Write incident reports you would be content to read aloud in a deposition, and fix the retention policy this month.
  • Founder → If you sell into frontier labs, expect procurement to shift toward vendors who can produce audit trails. Build the evidence layer now; it will be a line item in their S-1 controls narrative.
  • Investor → Treat lab safety disclosures as a declining information source. Price capability risk off third-party red-team results and government tests, not company blog posts.

THE CALL: Anthropic's IPO will be the moment AI safety stops being a brand and becomes a liability schedule. Expect the rhetoric to soften within two quarters of a filing.

SECTION 3 — MARKET MOVES

The Warsh Fed is tightening while a freight bellwether guides earnings down 5% to 10% (CNBC). That combination costs leveraged real-economy operators twice: volumes fall and refinancing gets dearer. JPMorgan abandoning its oil framework (BBC) is the honest version of the same problem — the inflation input is now set in Tehran and Washington, not in the data.

SECTION 5 — BOTTOM LINE

The limit on AI has moved from what models can do to what other people will allow: county boards, state governors, courts with subpoena power, and a Fed that decides what the buildout costs. Capability is no longer the scarce input — consent and capital are. Every 2027 plan built on cheaper money and quiet permitting needs rewriting.

SECTION 6 — WHAT DIDN'T MAKE THE CUT
  • Warren Buffett steps down as Berkshire chairman — a long-standing succession plan executing on schedule changes no allocation decision, however good the quotes.
  • Tilly Norwood's press tour, including an AI apparently switching into Chinese mid-interview — one malfunctioning synthetic actor is an anecdote, not a signal about synthetic media economics.
  • AllTrails and REI partner (100 million members versus 26 million) — a single co-marketing deal; the interesting version is app-first brands acquiring physical retail distribution, and one partnership doesn't establish it.
  • India requiring caller-ID apps to feed spam reports one-way to telcos, which Truecaller says hands over a proprietary asset — held for a future issue. Promote to a signal if a second large market (EU, Brazil, Indonesia) mandates the same one-way transfer, which would make user-generated data a compulsory public utility input and reset valuations across consumer data businesses.
