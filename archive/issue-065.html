SECTION 2 — SIGNALS

An energy shock is arriving with no rate-cut cushion underneath it

WHAT HAPPENED: Iran claimed strikes on American vessels and oil tankers around Hormuz in retaliation for US strikes, with Trump insisting prices fall fast once the war ends (CNBC). Brent is spiking and $100 is back in the conversation (NYT DealBook). Separately, Saudi Arabia and the Houthis are edging back toward open conflict after attacks on energy targets (NYT). Meanwhile inflation is rising globally and central banks are hiking again (The Economist), alongside a global bond sell-off (The Economist).

WHY THIS WEEK: The Hormuz exchange and the bond sell-off landed in the same week that policy rates are moving up rather than down. That ordering is what matters.

THE SECOND-ORDER READ: In 2022 an energy shock met central banks starting from zero, so the fiscal and monetary response was cheap. This one meets banks already tightening and a bond market demanding more term premium. The transmission runs through freight before it runs through pumps: Hormuz risk raises war-risk insurance, which raises charter rates, which lands in goods prices — the one inflation component central banks cannot credibly call transitory. For anyone financing anything, the discount rate moves before the revenue does, and long-duration cash flows get marked down first. CNBC's Investing Club already rotated defensively citing higher oil and Treasury yields (CNBC).

WHAT I'M NOT CERTAIN OF: The strike claim comes from Iran's own statements; I have no independent damage assessment and no evidence tanker transit volumes have actually fallen. I also have no Brent level or barrel figure in front of me — "spiking" is the source's word, not a number I can check.

WHAT TO DO:

  • Operator → reprice any 2027 contract with a freight or energy pass-through clause this month; if your supplier agreements are fixed-price with no surcharge cap, you are the hedge.
  • Founder → if you were planning to raise in Q1, pull it forward or extend runway to 24 months. Rate-hiking cycles close growth-stage windows on a lag.
  • Investor → assume the equity risk premium you underwrote in 2025 no longer clears. Re-run marks against a higher risk-free rate rather than waiting for the next round to tell you.

THE CALL: The Trump position that prices drop fast when the war ends may be true, but it is not investable. Underwrite the tightening, not the ceasefire.

SECTION 3 — MARKET MOVES

China's August wholesale inflation beat estimates, but on commodity costs and tech manufacturing demand plus a favourable base effect — not household demand, which came in on forecast (CNBC). Read it as imported energy inflation and export-facing factory orders, which means it tightens margins for anyone buying Chinese components without signalling a consumer recovery.

SECTION 5 — BOTTOM LINE

Everything physical is getting more expensive — energy, freight, metals, credit — at the same moment private software marks are expanding on the assumption that capital stays cheap and buyers stay acquisitive. Those two facts are financed by the same balance sheets, and only one of them can be right for long. The tell will be the first AI infrastructure project repriced for steel and power rather than for compute.
